## Purpose and constraints

These experiments convert the Research Director's five private-prototype hypotheses into behavioral evaluations. Every claim below is labeled **HYPOTHESIS**. The experiments do not change approved product direction, authorize a public alternative path, or create `DATA PROVEN` or `RUSS PROVEN` findings in advance.

All conditions must preserve the following **PRATER STANDARD** requirements:

- UNDERSTAND FIRST. ADVISE SECOND.
- Client Benefit Principle.
- Free text in the approved primary conversation.
- Direct answers when appropriate.
- One principal question at a time.
- Correction, skip, uncertainty, topic change, and immediate human continuation.
- Value before contact information.
- Professional boundaries.
- Separate affirmative consent before full-context transfer.
- Independent call/text when transfer is declined.
- No visitor-facing internal scores, labels, profiles, confidence, unknown lists, summaries, roadmaps, or reasoning.

No experiment may use increased disclosure, contact sharing, conversation length, completion, lack of objection, or consent acceptance as a standalone success measure.

## Shared evaluation protocol

Use synthetic scenarios followed by moderated sessions with representative private-test participants when authorized. Include early exploration, overlapping motives, repairs, financial strain, bereavement/inheritance, direct questions, correction, uncertainty, resume, and handoff.

Capture:

- participant explanation of what Russ understood;
- accuracy and correction success;
- felt pressure and ability to decline/skip;
- perceived relevance and usefulness;
- comprehension of limits, memory, and handoff scope;
- privacy surprise or concern;
- task success on mobile and with applicable accessibility modes;
- immediate comfort continuing and, when feasible, delayed comfort/regret;
- Russell's ability to continue without unnecessary repetition;
- complete behavior defects and adverse reactions, including non-completion.

Do not infer participant psychology, trust, or readiness from behavioral proxies alone. Combine observable task evidence with direct participant report and full conversation review.

## H-01 - Concise editable reflection plus one answer-linked question

**HYPOTHESIS:** A concise, editable reflection followed by one answer-linked question will increase felt understanding and reduce pressure compared with a question-only flow.

### Behavioral variants

- **Variant A - Reflection plus question:** After a meaningful disclosure, Russ paraphrases the decision-relevant meaning, makes correction easy, and asks one question whose relevance is visible.
- **Variant B - Question only:** Russ asks the same otherwise-compliant next question without the reflection. It may use a neutral transition but no generic pseudo-empathy.

Both variants must answer direct questions first and may decide not to ask a question when direction is more useful. The test concerns eligible acknowledgment moments, not a mandatory response template.

### Representative tasks

- low mortgage rate plus need for space;
- inherited property with multiple owners;
- job relocation plus buy-before-sell concern;
- visitor correction after an inaccurate reflection;
- visitor who says "I don't know."

### Evidence supporting the hypothesis

- Participants more accurately identify what Russ understood.
- Participants report greater felt understanding and lower pressure without lower comprehension or usefulness.
- Corrections are made earlier/easier and govern later responses.
- Full interaction review shows fewer unnecessary repeated questions.
- Benefits persist across sensitive and nonsensitive scenarios without increased privacy surprise.

### Evidence challenging the hypothesis

- Reflections feel repetitive, presumptive, slow, manipulative, or emotionally exaggerated.
- Misinterpretations increase or participants correct less often.
- Direct answers are delayed or the conversation becomes formulaic.
- Pressure, privacy concern, or drop-off rises in meaningful subgroups/scenarios.
- Any apparent benefit exists only as more disclosure or longer conversations.

### Decision rule

Do not adopt a fixed reflection cadence. If supported, document the bounded contexts in which reflection adds benefit and the failure cases in which Russ should answer, direct, or pause instead.

## H-02 - Client-benefit rationale before a consequential question

**HYPOTHESIS:** A one-sentence client-benefit rationale before a consequential question will improve perceived fairness and informed willingness without increasing regret.

### Behavioral variants

- **Variant A - Brief rationale:** Russ names why the answer would help the visitor before asking one consequential, sensitive, or surprising question.
- **Variant B - No rationale:** Russ asks the same otherwise-compliant question directly.

Do not test rationales that disguise a collection purpose, overpromise benefit, or pressure agreement. Ordinary low-risk questions should not receive needless explanations.

### Representative tasks

- asking about a real deadline during financial pressure;
- asking whether other owners must participate in an inherited-property decision;
- asking which cost/risk matters in buy-before-sell planning;
- requesting minimum contact information after "Ask Russell to contact me";
- verifying stale context on resume.

### Evidence supporting the hypothesis

- Participants can explain why the question was asked and how answering might help.
- Perceived relevance, fairness, control, and comfort improve.
- Skip/decline remains easy and participants do not report greater obligation.
- Delayed regret and privacy concern do not increase.
- Answers are no less accurate, and Russ does not collect more than needed.

### Evidence challenging the hypothesis

- The rationale feels like persuasion, justification theater, or a cue to the desired answer.
- It increases pressure, response length, or sensitive disclosure without improved comprehension.
- Participants misunderstand the actual use, recipient, or retention of the information.
- Routine explanations make the conversation cumbersome or scripted.

### Decision rule

If supported, restrict rationales to question types where participants demonstrably benefit. The experiment cannot justify explaining every question or treating disclosure as success.

## H-03 - Free text plus optional structured controls

**HYPOTHESIS:** A hybrid of free text, optional structured controls, and visible back/correction will outperform pure chat and a conventional multi-field form on comprehension, comfort, and mobile task success.

### Behavioral variants

- **Variant A - Hybrid approved-path prototype:** Free text is primary; optional quick replies reduce effort; back/correction is visible; controls never constrain the response.
- **Variant B - Pure chat comparator:** Free text only, with equivalent behavior content and correction capability.
- **Variant C - Structured research comparator:** A conventional multi-field form representing the same decision content, used only in research evaluation. It is not an approved second product path.

Randomize order where feasible and keep substantive guidance, reading level, privacy notice, continuation access, and professional boundaries equivalent.

### Representative tasks

- initial "thinking about selling" exploration;
- multiple overlapping motives that do not fit one category;
- skip and "I don't know";
- correction of a prior selection;
- mobile keyboard, 320 CSS pixel, zoom, screen reader, and speech-input use as applicable.

### Evidence supporting the hypothesis

- Hybrid participants complete intended tasks with equal or better comprehension, comfort, correction success, and accessibility/mobile usability.
- Optional controls reduce effort without suppressing free-text nuance or forcing categories.
- Participants notice and successfully use back/correction.
- The hybrid does not increase pressure, accidental selection, or privacy surprise.
- Benefits appear in task quality and user report, not merely completion speed or answer volume.

### Evidence challenging the hypothesis

- Quick replies anchor answers, erase overlapping motives, or are mistaken for required choices.
- Controls reduce candor, increase correction burden, or create accessibility failures.
- Pure chat or form comparator performs better on comprehension, comfort, or task success.
- Hybrid complexity adds navigation cost without a client benefit.

### Decision rule

Retain free text in the approved product path. Revise or remove structured controls that do not reduce effort without constraining meaning. Comparator results inform design; they do not silently authorize a form-first product direction.

## H-04 - Paraphrased, confirmable memory

**HYPOTHESIS:** Paraphrased and confirmable memory will preserve continuity with less privacy concern than verbatim recall; explicit uncertainty will outperform confidently wrong recall.

### Behavioral variants

- **Variant A - Paraphrased and confirmable:** Russ references only the relevant prior meaning and offers correction.
- **Variant B - Verbatim:** Russ repeats an equivalent prior statement verbatim, with safeguards excluding gratuitously harmful content.
- **Variant C - Explicit uncertainty:** For stale/conflicting information, Russ states uncertainty and verifies.
- **Variant D - Confidently wrong:** Use only in controlled synthetic evaluation or carefully consented testing with immediate repair; do not expose vulnerable participants to material harm.

All variants must preserve actual correction precedence after the test moment.

### Representative tasks

- return after a browser/session break;
- remembered selling timeline now stale;
- corrected number/date;
- sensitive bereavement, divorce, or financial context;
- visitor starts on a potentially shared device/screen.

### Evidence supporting the hypothesis

- Paraphrase provides equal or better continuity with lower privacy surprise/concern than verbatim recall.
- Participants identify and correct remembered information more easily.
- Explicit uncertainty produces greater accuracy, comfort, and trust calibration than confident error.
- Subsequent responses and handoff materials consistently use corrected/current information.
- Resume reduces repetition without replaying more context than needed.

### Evidence challenging the hypothesis

- Paraphrase changes meaning, hides provenance, or creates more correction work.
- Participants prefer exact wording for particular nonsensitive facts and can explain why.
- Explicit uncertainty becomes repetitive or undermines useful continuity when memory is reliable/current.
- Privacy concern remains high because the issue is retention/audience, not wording.

### Decision rule

Do not generalize one memory style to all facts. Document bounded rules by sensitivity, recency, conflict, and consequence. Confident wrong recall is never adopted; it is a failure reference condition.

## H-05 - Preview-and-approve full-context handoff

**HYPOTHESIS:** A preview-and-approve handoff that explains recipient, scope, purpose, and alternatives will produce better comprehension and comfort than a generic "connect" prompt, while allowing Russell to continue without re-asking.

### Behavioral variants

- **Variant A - Preview and approve:** Names Russell, ordered conversation scope, corrections/confirmation state, purpose, optional supplemental summary, and independent call/text alternative; requires a separate affirmative action.
- **Variant B - Generic connect comparator:** Uses a concise generic connection prompt for research comparison only. It must not actually transfer context without the approved informed consent flow; use a simulated choice or follow it with the compliant notice before any real transfer.

The approved product behavior remains Variant A regardless of acceptance-rate differences unless the Product Owner changes policy.

### Representative tasks

- visitor chooses Ask Russell to contact me after a substantive conversation;
- visitor independently calls/texts and declines transfer;
- visitor believes only a summary will be shared;
- visitor agrees, then a technical failure prevents transfer;
- Russell reviews full ordered context plus a clearly marked supplemental summary.

### Evidence supporting the hypothesis

- Participants accurately explain recipient, scope, purpose, summary status, and alternatives in their own words.
- Comfort and perceived control are equal or better without increased pressure or regret.
- Decline is easy and independent call/text remains usable.
- Russell can continue without unnecessary repetition and can find corrections/nuance.
- Transfer failures are understood and recovered from without false claims.

### Evidence challenging the hypothesis

- Participants still believe only a summary is shared or misunderstand who receives the content.
- Preview length overwhelms, consent controls steer agreement, or decline feels punitive.
- Russell cannot efficiently use the package, re-asks known facts, or overlooks corrections.
- Acceptance rises while comprehension, comfort, or delayed regret worsens.
- Full context creates avoidable privacy or information-overload harms that require a policy/design review.

### Decision rule

Optimize first for informed comprehension, control, continuity, and low unnecessary repetition. Do not optimize for consent acceptance. Any future proposal for per-message/per-topic controls is a Product Owner decision triggered by evidence, not an assumption in this experiment.

## Evidence classification after evaluation

- A single usability session or scenario pass remains evaluation evidence, not `RUSS PROVEN`.
- Quantitative prototype observations may become `DATA PROVEN` only after data quality, population, measures, and limitations are documented and approved for the bounded claim.
- A Russ behavior may become `RUSS PROVEN` only after repeated representative evaluation across relevant scenarios and versions, including adverse cases and regression evidence.
- Null, mixed, and harmful findings must be retained; they are not failures to be discarded.
