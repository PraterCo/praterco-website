# Prater Conversion Design System

## Purpose

Build digital experiences that earn trust quickly, make the next step obvious, and feel unmistakably like the business behind them.

Prater & Co. is the first implementation and the proving ground for a reusable framework that can later be adapted to other brands and industries.

## Version 1 outcome

A homeowner spends roughly 10 to 15 minutes with the experience and leaves thinking:

- He understood me.
- I trust Russell.
- I want to meet Russell.

## Core rules

1. **One primary purpose at a time**  
   Every page or state must make the next action obvious.

2. **Conversation is the main path**  
   Visitors should never have to hunt for the place to begin talking.

3. **Understand before advising**  
   The experience follows the meaningful thing the visitor just said before moving to a new topic.

4. **The homeowner never sees internal reasoning**  
   Readiness scores, unknowns, internal summaries, diagnostic labels, and roadmaps belong in Russell's private view, not the public experience.

5. **Progressive disclosure**  
   Reveal information as it becomes useful. Do not present the entire system at once.

6. **Human before technology**  
   The interface should feel like a conversation with Russell, not a software demonstration.

7. **Every significant decision is documented**  
   Record the rule, supporting research, alternatives considered, implementation, and later test results.

## Prater brand expression

### Personality

- Warm
- Conversational
- Confident without ego
- Direct but not pushy
- Patient
- Patriotic
- Community-minded

### Visual direction

- Deep navy for trust and stability
- Rich red for action and warmth
- White and warm neutral space for clarity
- Selective gold accents for heritage and achievement
- More color than the current experience, without making the site loud or novelty-themed

### Character

The existing Prater logo character is the visual foundation for Russ. Any future face, avatar, illustration, or animation should evolve from that character rather than introduce an unrelated identity.

## Public experience path

### 1. Arrival

Purpose: Help the visitor feel comfortable and make the conversation easy to find.

Primary action: Start talking.

### 2. Conversation

Purpose: Let Russell's natural cadence carry the experience.

Primary action: Continue the conversation.

### 3. Understanding

Purpose: Show the visitor that Russ accurately heard what matters.

Primary action: Confirm or clarify.

### 4. Recommendation

Purpose: Provide a useful first direction without overwhelming the visitor.

Primary action: Review the recommended next step.

### 5. Connection

Purpose: Make speaking with Russell feel like the natural continuation of the conversation.

Primary action: Call, schedule, or send the conversation to Russell.

## Initial homepage direction

- Clear, inviting headline
- Visible Russ character based on the logo
- One unmistakable path into chat
- Supporting navigation remains available but visually secondary
- Navy, white, red, and restrained gold palette
- Friendly language that invites a real estate question without forcing the visitor into a category first

## Initial seller experience direction

Remove the public-facing decision dashboard, including:

- Decision readiness
- Exploring / Clarifying / Planning labels
- Here's what I'm hearing panel during the live conversation
- What's still unclear
- Seller roadmap
- Internal decision profile language

Retain the underlying information privately so it can help Russell prepare for the follow-up.

The public seller experience should be dominated by the conversation, followed by a concise confirmation and a clear next step.

## Reusable framework structure

For future sites, separate the system into three layers:

1. **Conversion rules** — research-backed principles that apply across industries
2. **Brand expression** — voice, colors, imagery, audience, trust signals, and personality
3. **Component patterns** — reusable heroes, chat entry points, forms, testimonials, scheduling, summaries, and calls to action

## Decision record template

For each major design decision, document:

- Decision
- User problem
- Research or evidence
- Alternatives considered
- Brand interpretation
- Implementation location
- Metric to watch
- Test result
- Status: proposed, adopted, validated, revised, or retired
